Late-romantic symphony orchestra, warm analogue recording from the 1970s, wide
dynamic range, no brickwall compression. Full orchestra only: strings, woodwinds,
horns, trombones, timpani, harp, celesta, concert grand piano, nylon-string Spanish
guitar. Cellos and double basses carry a continuous walking eighth-note line, front
and centre as a lead voice. Cor anglais and oboe take the long singing melodies.
Pipe organ doubles the brass in the chorale. No drum kit, no electric bass, no
Hammond organ, no Moog, no synthesiser of any kind.
Meters alternate 5/4 and 6/4. B-flat major with Phrygian colour.

One section, a finale of fifty-five seconds.

Full tutti at quarter = 63, a plagal cadence, grand but warm rather than loud. Every
instrument plays together. Two melodies that have been kept apart are stated
together for the first time. A baritone sings four lines over the tutti, plain and
conversational, sitting inside the orchestra rather than on top of it. The ending
resolves gently, one note settling down a half step; not triumphant, not a fanfare.
Let it come to rest.
