1970s symphonic progressive rock, analog and warm, tape saturation, wide dynamic range, no modern brickwall compression.
Rickenbacker bass played with a pick, front and centre as a lead voice. Nylon-string Spanish guitar, concert grand piano, strings, Hammond organ through a Leslie cabinet, Moog synthesiser lead, palmas hand claps and cajon. No drum kit anywhere.
Meters alternate 5/4 and 6/4. B-flat major with Phrygian colour. Long developed instrumental writing; vocals are rare and sit inside the band.

Three sections in order.

First, Flamenco. Starts almost empty at quarter = 76 and builds in three steps to 96. Nylon-string guitar and bass lead. Palmas and cajon carry a 12-beat compas. Andalusian cadence Gm-F-Eb-D. Density grows from nothing to full. A Moog lead appears only in the last fifth. No vocals.

Second, a short bridge, fifteen seconds, quarter = 100. Piano and bass. Two melodies overlap and clash slightly; do not resolve the dissonance. One short vocal line.

Third, the quietest music in the piece, quarter = 56, very slow. Solo piano with sparse bass underneath, long silences between phrases. Air and space matter more than notes. No guitar. No vocals.
